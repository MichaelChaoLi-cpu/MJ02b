## 1. Research Objective

### Central Research Question

- Research question: How should a proposed district-targeted occupational humid-heat protection program in Cambodia prioritize locations and socially vulnerable workers under limited implementation capacity and uncertainty in the available evidence?
- Why it matters: A uniform national response can allocate scarce protective resources to places with different humid-heat burdens, worker populations, and adjustment capacities. An ex ante social impact assessment can make the distributional consequences of alternative targeting rules visible before implementation.
- Data support currently visible: Nine survey waves provide 179,217 working-age person-wave observations linked to 3,325 district-year-month climate cells across 197 current districts. The completed analysis supplies two cumulative humid-heat definitions, a work-participation association, an education-related vulnerability contrast, spatial heat clustering, survey weights, geographic coverage, and a transparent multiplicity and stability audit.
- Key readable variables or data scope: Two-Month Cumulative Extreme Humid-Heat Days, Two-Month Average Maximum Wet-Bulb Temperature, Work Participation, Low Education, Person Weight, District, Survey Wave, exposure coverage, fixed-effect-residual exposure variation, and candidate district-level policy-screening constructs derived from these approved variables.
- What would support it: Objective-aligned policy-allocation strategies produce meaningful trade-offs in Humid-Heat Coverage, Vulnerable-Worker Coverage, Working-Age Coverage, and geographic reach; the combined strategy occupies a nondominated or substantively defensible compromise position without being assumed superior in advance; and a stable subset of districts remains prioritized across reasonable weights, exposure definitions, capacity definitions, samples, and geographic assumptions.
- What would falsify or weaken it: One strategy dominates only because its ranking quantity is mechanically embedded in every assessment criterion, the combined strategy is dominated under the prespecified criteria, priority districts change radically across validated exposure definitions or capacity definitions, or the available survey weights and geographic support cannot produce defensible district-level worker and education summaries.
- Required next feasibility check: Verify that every proposed district indicator can be constructed from read-only database inputs, define the admissible policy alternatives and budget units, test whether weighted district summaries have adequate effective sample support, and determine whether prospective program-effect scenarios can be reported only as transparent assumptions rather than estimated causal benefits.

### Supporting Research Questions

#### Supporting Point 1: Baseline humid-heat labor impact evidence

- Role relative to central point: establish the social-impact baseline.
- Research question: What spatial and temporal pattern of cumulative humid heat is observed across Cambodia, and what exploratory association does that exposure have with work participation?
- Why it matters: An impact-assessment framework needs an empirically grounded hazard and social-outcome pathway before it can compare intervention locations.
- Data support currently visible: The validated two-month threshold exposure averages about 23.7 days and retains a 4.10-day residual standard deviation across 3,114 complete district-month cells. Threshold and continuous cumulative exposure estimates are adverse and directionally stable, but no eligible test survives global 10% false-discovery control.
- Key readable variables or data scope: Extreme Humid-Heat Days, Five-Day Cumulative Humid-Heat Exposure, Two-Month Average Maximum Wet-Bulb Temperature, Work Participation, Monthly Rainfall, District Calendar Month, Survey Year, and Person Weight.
- What would support it: Both cumulative definitions remain adverse under the frozen model and sensitivity contract and the spatial exposure summaries retain sufficient coverage for district screening.
- What would falsify or weaken it: The cumulative directions reverse under the frozen checks, future exposure reproduces the full pattern, or district exposure support is too sparse for a national screening exercise.
- Required feasibility check: Preserve the existing association estimates as exploratory baseline evidence and verify that the district screening layer never represents them as district-specific or causal program effects.

#### Supporting Point 2: Distributional vulnerability and equity

- Role relative to central point: identify affected groups and distributional consequences.
- Research question: Which districts combine relatively high cumulative humid heat with greater concentrations of low-education working-age people, and how would alternative targeting rules distribute protection across education groups?
- Why it matters: Social impact assessment requires attention to who benefits, who remains uncovered, and whether apparently efficient allocation reproduces existing social disadvantage.
- Data support currently visible: Harmonized education is available for 154,208 person-wave observations, both cumulative exposure definitions show a more adverse low-education interaction, and the completed spatial analysis provides district heat and survey-weighted education summaries. Global bivariate heat-education co-location is unsupported, so local distributions must be evaluated without claiming a national spatial relationship.
- Key readable variables or data scope: Low Education, Person Weight, District, Work Participation, the two cumulative humid-heat measures, weighted district working-age population, and candidate vulnerable-worker coverage measures.
- What would support it: Humid-heat-burden and vulnerable-worker targeting reach different education compositions, and a combined or equity-oriented rule increases coverage of low-education workers while retaining substantial humid-heat coverage.
- What would falsify or weaken it: District education shares are too imprecise, equity-oriented targeting merely reproduces the hazard-only list, or conclusions depend on unsupported local causal-effect estimates.
- Required feasibility check: Establish district-level effective sample sizes and uncertainty for weighted Low Education shares, define minimum-support rules, and test alternative shrinkage or exclusion rules without searching for a more favorable subgroup.

#### Supporting Point 3: Policy alternatives, capacity, and expected consequences

- Role relative to central point: compare proposed actions.
- Research question: Under limited District-count and worker-share capacity, how do no additional targeting, uniform coverage, humid-heat-burden-first, vulnerable-worker-first, worker-scale-first, and combined targeting strategies differ in Humid-Heat Coverage, Vulnerable-Worker Coverage, Working-Age Coverage, and geographic reach?
- Why it matters: The practical consequence of an impact assessment is not one risk map but a transparent comparison of feasible action alternatives and the people or places each alternative includes or excludes.
- Data support currently visible: District identifiers, current-boundary polygons, cumulative heat, survey weights, work participation, education, survey-wave coverage, and identifying-support diagnostics can support relative allocation scenarios. Actual program costs and causal intervention effectiveness are not currently observed.
- Key readable variables or data scope: District Threshold Humid-Heat Burden, District Continuous Humid-Heat Burden, District Vulnerable-Worker Scale, Weighted Working-Age Scale, Data Support Grade, Targeting Strategy, Capacity Level, Humid-Heat Coverage, Vulnerable-Worker Coverage, and Working-Age Coverage.
- What would support it: The objective-aligned policy alternatives generate interpretable trade-offs, each single-objective strategy maximizes its corresponding coverage criterion at a fixed District capacity, the combined strategy provides a transparent compromise if it is nondominated, and results remain interpretable under matched worker-share capacity targets.
- What would falsify or weaken it: All alternatives produce effectively identical allocations, equal-District and worker-share capacity definitions lead to incompatible recommendations, or the combined strategy is dominated by a single-objective strategy across all reported criteria.
- Required feasibility check: Define each action alternative before estimation, identify whether capacity should be measured by districts, weighted workers, or both, and report any program-effectiveness parameter only as an externally specified sensitivity range rather than an estimated intervention effect.

#### Supporting Point 4: Decision robustness and uncertainty

- Role relative to central point: test whether the proposed decision support is stable enough to use.
- Research question: Which districts and policy conclusions remain stable across reasonable indicator weights, cumulative humid-heat definitions, capacity levels, Survey Wave exclusions, geographic restrictions, Data Support Grade eligibility rules, and wave-composition uncertainty?
- Why it matters: A high-stakes priority list is not credible if small analytic choices replace most selected districts or reverse the equity conclusion.
- Data support currently visible: The completed research includes two cumulative exposure definitions, leave-one-wave-out estimates, balanced-district and coastal-fallback restrictions, current-boundary polygons, sampling weights, and multiplicity-adjusted evidence grades.
- Key readable variables or data scope: District Priority Score, Priority Inclusion Frequency, Humid-Heat Coverage, Vulnerable-Worker Coverage, Working-Age Coverage, Targeting Strategy, Capacity Level, Survey Wave, Coastal Climate Fallback, and Data Support Grade.
- What would support it: A core set of districts has high inclusion frequency, aggregate coverage and equity conclusions retain direction, and reported uncertainty identifies rather than conceals unstable borderline districts.
- What would falsify or weaken it: No stable priority core exists, Priority Inclusion Frequency remains low across the two validated exposure definitions, or aggregate coverage and equity conclusions reverse across ordinary wave-composition or weighting perturbations.
- Required feasibility check: Prespecify an admissible weight simplex and perturbation set, choose a reproducible resampling procedure compatible with survey waves and shared district-month exposure, and define stability thresholds before viewing the final rankings.

### Scope of Analysis

- Topics: ex ante social impact assessment, occupational humid-heat protection, spatial targeting, labor participation, education-related vulnerability, distributional equity, policy capacity, and decision uncertainty.
- Proposed action: A district-targeted occupational humid-heat protection program combining humidity-aware alerts, work-rest-water-shade guidance, schedule flexibility, and targeted communication for workers with fewer educational resources. The study evaluates targeting and potential coverage, not the realized effectiveness of this package.
- Policy alternatives: no additional targeting; uniform national provision; humid-heat-burden-first targeting; vulnerable-worker-first targeting; worker-scale-first targeting; and combined three-objective targeting. These are allocation rules for a common protection package, not different intervention packages.
- Capacity scenarios: primary illustrative selection of 20, 40, and 60 current Districts, with 40 Districts designated as the central display scenario, supplemented by required worker-share capacity targets of 10%, 20%, and 30% of Weighted Working-Age Scale.
- Population: people aged 15-64 with a positive analysis weight and a valid district-year-month exposure match.
- Units of analysis: person-wave for the baseline labor association; district for hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and priority screening; policy-strategy-by-capacity scenario for alternative comparison.
- Period: nine survey waves in 2007, 2009, 2011, 2013, 2014, 2016, 2017, 2019, and 2021. The empirical baseline is historical; no future climate projection is assumed at the research-question stage.
- Geography: 197 current Cambodian districts or municipalities, with explicit minimum-support rules, balanced-coverage checks, and the documented coastal climate fallback sensitivity.
- Primary evidence boundary: The impact assessment is a prospective decision-support exercise built on exploratory observational associations. It does not estimate the causal effect of humid heat, a causal district-specific labor loss, or the realized benefit or cost of the proposed protection program.
- Secondary evidence boundary: Hours, wages, sex, age, rural residence, occupation, crowding, and electricity remain unsupported mechanisms or modifiers and will not enter the priority index unless a later human-approved feasibility revision provides an independent rationale.
- Directions outside the current redesign: future climate projections, monetized cost-benefit analysis, employer compliance behavior, daily interview-date designs, health outcomes, and program-effectiveness estimation from an actual intervention.

### Study Design Declaration

- Research type: applied
- Study design: Ex ante spatial social impact assessment and multi-criteria decision analysis built on a survey-weighted repeated-cross-section observational study.
- Baseline evidence design: Compare working-age people observed in the same District and calendar Survey Month across survey years with different realized two-month cumulative humid-heat burdens, retaining the frozen controls, fixed effects, weighting, clustering, timing, and multiplicity contract.
- Decision design: Aggregate approved humid-heat burden, vulnerable-worker scale, working-age scale, and data-support indicators to District; compare objective-aligned policy-allocation alternatives under District-count and worker-share capacity constraints; and evaluate coverage, distributional equity, geographic reach, and ranking stability.
- Interpretation limit: Report priority screening and scenario consequences under explicit assumptions. Do not use causal policy-benefit language, treat wet-bulb temperature as WBGT, interpret local clusters as district-specific effects, or conceal the global multiplicity result.

## 2. Theoretical Background  /  Conceptual Framework  /  Problem Formulation

Research type: applied

Section focus: A decision-facing social impact assessment of where a proposed occupational humid-heat protection program should be prioritized, who would be covered or omitted, and how stable those choices are under evidence uncertainty.

### Research Gap

- Existing heat-labor studies generally estimate average exposure-outcome relationships, while implementation decisions require explicit comparison of where limited protective capacity should be deployed and which groups would benefit.
- Heat-vulnerability maps often combine hazard and demographic indicators without validating whether the hazard is associated with a relevant social outcome, separating exposure from vulnerability, or testing whether priority rankings survive alternative reasonable specifications.
- The completed Cambodia evidence supplies a labor-outcome pathway and an education-related vulnerability contrast, but it does not yet translate those findings into a formal impact-assessment comparison of current or proposed actions.
- A useful social impact assessment must remain informative when evidence is imperfect. Here, exact interview days are unavailable, the central association is exploratory, global false-discovery control is not passed, and intervention effectiveness and costs are not observed. These limitations motivate decision robustness and transparent scenarios rather than stronger causal language.
- The international novelty claim and the program-effectiveness scenario range require later literature and official-guidance review; they are not established by the current data alone.

### Conceptual Framework

The framework separates four district-level components:

1. Humid-heat burden: working-age scale multiplied by cumulative humid-heat intensity.
2. Working-age reach: the survey-weighted working-age population potentially reached by district protection.
3. Vulnerable-worker scale: working-age scale multiplied by the District Low-Education Share.
4. Evidence support: survey-wave coverage, effective sample support, and the stability of exposure measurement.

For district \(g\), an illustrative multi-criteria priority score under weighting scenario \(w\) is

\[
P_g(w) = w_B R(B_g) + w_E R(E_g) + w_L R(L_g),
\qquad w_B + w_E + w_L = 1,
\]

where \(B_g\) is working-age-weighted humid-heat burden, \(E_g\) is Weighted Working-Age Scale, \(L_g=E_gV_g\) is District Vulnerable-Worker Scale, \(V_g\) is District Low-Education Share, \(R(\cdot)\) is the prespecified percentile transformation, and \(w_B\), \(w_E\), and \(w_L\) are nonnegative weights. Evidence support remains a separate uncertainty flag and does not enter the score. No single weighting vector or combined strategy is treated as objectively superior.

Each policy strategy selects a set \(A_s(k)\) under strategy \(s\) and capacity \(k\). Its consequences are compared through Humid-Heat Coverage, Vulnerable-Worker Coverage, Working-Age Coverage, geographic reach, and Priority Inclusion Frequency. The exploratory Work Participation association supports the plausibility and direction of the impact pathway but is not converted into district-specific labor loss, avoided impact, or causal program benefit.

The impact pathway is

\[
\text{Cumulative humid-heat hazard}
\rightarrow \text{potential worker exposure}
\rightarrow \text{labor-participation risk signal}
\rightarrow \text{unequal vulnerability}
\rightarrow \text{targeting alternative}
\rightarrow \text{coverage and equity consequences}.
\]

The proposed program is expected to influence the last link only if alerts, rest, water, shade, schedule flexibility, and targeted communication are implemented and effective. Their effectiveness is not identified by the observational survey.

### Problem Formulation

Let \(i\) index people, \(g\) districts, \(m\) calendar months, \(y\) nominal survey-wave years, \(s\) policy strategies, and \(k\) implementation-capacity levels. The existing person-level model supplies exploratory evidence about the association between cumulative humid heat and Work Participation and its difference by Low Education. The new impact-assessment layer converts only approved district summaries into prespecified action alternatives.

The revised analytical sequence is:

1. preserve the frozen cumulative humid-heat, work-participation, education, timing, spatial, and multiplicity evidence as the empirical baseline;
2. validate district-level humid-heat burden, worker-scale, vulnerable-worker-scale, and data-support indicators;
3. define the proposed protection package, action alternatives, capacity units, evaluation criteria, and uncertainty set before producing priority rankings;
4. compare no additional targeting, uniform provision, and objective-aligned targeted strategies on Humid-Heat Coverage, Vulnerable-Worker Coverage, Working-Age Coverage, geographic reach, and decision stability under both District-count and worker-share capacity definitions;
5. identify stable priority districts through alternative exposure definitions, admissible weights, capacity levels, sampling perturbations, wave exclusions, and geographic restrictions;
6. report excluded populations, unstable borderline districts, unsupported mechanisms, and global multiplicity limits alongside the recommended allocation strategy;
7. translate the results into a tiered protection and monitoring recommendation without claiming that the program itself was evaluated in practice.

The constructive claim is conditional: the study can identify robust allocation priorities and transparent trade-offs if the district indicators and ranking core remain stable. If they do not, the valid output is a decision-uncertainty map and a statement that national targeting requires better local data, not a forced priority list.

- Survey component: harmonized Cambodia Socio-Economic Survey repeated cross-sections with employment, demographics, education, household composition, housing, expenditure, geography, and released sampling weights.
- Climate component: daily gridded reanalysis for air temperature, wet-bulb temperature, apparent temperature, and precipitation, aggregated to district-calendar-month records.
- Geography component: a current administrative boundary crosswalk linking survey sampling areas to districts and district climate locations.
- Linkage key: Survey Year, Survey Month, and District. Person and household identifiers remain unique only within Survey Wave.
- Access contract: analytical code obtains all research inputs from PostgreSQL with `SELECT` under a read-only transaction. No research-analysis step writes to the database.

### Exposure Coverage and Variation

- The climate layer contains 16,824 published district-month records spanning 2007-2021 and the survey-relevant years and districts.
- The 3,325 district-month cells appearing in the person-level analytical sample average 11.08 Extreme Humid-Heat Days per month, with a standard deviation of 10.18, a median of 10, and a range from 0 to 31.
- Approximately 26.6% of analytical district-month cells have zero days above the primary wet-bulb threshold.
- After removing district-by-calendar-month and survey-year fixed effects, the residual standard deviation is 2.64 days for current-month Extreme Humid-Heat Days and 4.10 days for Two-Month Cumulative Extreme Humid-Heat Days across the 3,114 cells with valid current and preceding-month exposure. The 4.10-day cumulative residual variation is the basis of the primary estimate.
- Valid preceding-month climate matches permit construction of the two-month cumulative exposure for 167,902 person-wave observations. The lower count relative to the current-month sample is reported explicitly in every cumulative model.
- The threshold-based cumulative measure totals days with daily maximum wet-bulb temperature at least 26 C across the matched and preceding months. The continuous replication measure averages the two monthly means of daily maximum wet-bulb temperature.
- Severe Humid-Heat Days at 28 C are rare: 98.9% of analytical district-month cells have zero such days. This threshold is diagnostic rather than primary.
- Extreme Dry-Heat Days average 3.01 per month and are used only to test whether humid heat is more informative than dry temperature alone. Historical local-percentile thresholds are unavailable because the climate baseline spans only 1-8 usable years rather than the required 25 years.

### Outcome and Modifier Coverage

- Work Participation is observed for 99.999% of the analytical sample and is the primary outcome.
- Weekly Hours Including Nonworkers is observed for 76.1% of person-wave rows, with low coverage in 2011, 2016, 2019, and 2021. Hours models therefore include observation-selection and high-coverage-wave checks.
- Log Monthly Wage is observed for 41.6% of rows and is unavailable in 2007. Wage estimates are secondary, conditional on reporting, and use survey-year fixed effects to absorb common nominal-price differences.
- Harmonized education is available for 154,208 person-wave rows.
- The cumulative-exposure education-interaction sample contains 144,539 person-wave rows after requiring both preceding-month climate and harmonized education.
- Occupation is unavailable in 2007 and otherwise nearly complete among current workers. Occupation analyses condition on current work and cannot explain entry into employment.
- Household electricity, water, rooms, floor area, sanitation, and drinking-water variables cover almost the full sample. Household cost models use one record per household-wave to avoid duplicating household outcomes across members.

### Data Quality and Interpretation Limits

- The data are repeated cross-sections, not a person or household panel.
- Exact interview day is unavailable for most waves. Nominal Survey Year and released Survey Month are the frozen temporal match.
- The 2011-12 release is represented by nominal year 2011 under the same rule used for all primary estimates.
- Geography uses a current-boundary crosswalk rather than historically versioned district polygons.
- Climate represents a gridded district location at the centroid; 1,598 person-wave rows use a documented land representative-point fallback for a coastal district. Excluding these rows is a robustness check.
- District-month exposure is shared by all respondents in the same cell, so effective exposure variation is much smaller than the person-row count.
- Electricity spending is not direct evidence of cooling access. Detailed roof and wall source categories are not used as final moderators until a cross-wave material concordance is independently validated.
- Log Monthly Wage remains a nominal, selectively observed measure and is not the sole basis for welfare conclusions.
- The cumulative story was selected from an 82-row exploratory audit. Global and family-level false-discovery adjustments apply to 64 eligible substantive or exploratory tests; the remaining 18 leave-one-wave-out rows are stability diagnostics outside the adjustment. No eligible test survives global 10% false-discovery control; family-level adjusted results are descriptive evidence for prioritizing validation, not post hoc confirmation.

## 5. Identification Strategy

### Design Principle

The analysis has two linked but distinct layers. The frozen person-level layer supplies an exploratory social-impact pathway: it estimates whether two-month cumulative humid heat is associated with Work Participation and whether that association differs by Low Education. The district-level layer is an ex ante allocation exercise: it uses objective-aligned humid-heat burden, vulnerable-worker scale, working-age reach, and evidence support to compare where a common proposed protection package could be prioritized under limited capacity.

The person-level design compares working-age people observed in the same District and calendar Survey Month across Survey Years with different realized cumulative humid-heat burdens. District Calendar Month fixed effects absorb time-invariant local conditions and typical seasonality, while Survey Year fixed effects absorb national shocks common to a survey-wave year. The estimates remain associational because exact interview days, individual heat exposure, and an implemented protection program are unavailable.

The district-level design identifies relative allocation priorities rather than a causal treatment effect. District Threshold Humid-Heat Burden is the primary working-age-weighted historical burden. District Continuous Humid-Heat Burden is a replication burden based on nonnegative wet-bulb-temperature excess above the same 26 C reference used by the threshold exposure; it is not the product of worker scale and absolute Celsius temperature. Weighted Working-Age Scale represents potential reach; District Vulnerable-Worker Scale represents the relative low-education working-age population; and Data Support Grade records evidence limitations. The underlying hazard and Low Education share remain visible diagnostic components. Data Support Grade is never included in District Priority Score. It is displayed, used to define sensitivity samples, and considered when describing the confidence of a recommendation.

The objective-aligned priority rules, capacity levels, evaluation criteria, and uncertainty checks were revised after independent output critiques identified an asymmetric strategy comparison, an origin-dependent continuous burden that nearly duplicated worker scale, and a bootstrap that mixed specification uncertainty with wave-composition uncertainty. The revised analysis does not search over additional outcomes, subgroups, thresholds, or score components to obtain a preferred policy conclusion, and no strategy is designated superior before regeneration.

### Policy Alternatives and Capacity Contract

- A0, no additional targeting: select no District and use zero coverage as the lower reference point.
- A1, uniform national provision: include all 197 Districts and use full national coverage as an upper reference point. This is not represented as feasible under the limited-capacity scenarios.
- A2, humid-heat-burden-first targeting: rank Districts by District Threshold Humid-Heat Burden in the primary analysis. Repeat A2 with the nonnegative 26 C wet-bulb-excess District Continuous Humid-Heat Burden only as a prespecified replication. The primary rule directly maximizes Humid-Heat Coverage at a fixed District count.
- A3, vulnerable-worker-first targeting: rank Districts by District Vulnerable-Worker Scale. This rule directly maximizes Vulnerable-Worker Coverage at a fixed District count.
- A4, worker-scale-first targeting: rank Districts by Weighted Working-Age Scale.
- A5, combined targeting: rank Districts by the equal-weight District Priority Score as the transparent compromise rule and use the prespecified pure and emphasis weights as decision sensitivities. Do not label A5 as preferred unless the completed criteria and robustness results support that conclusion.

The primary Capacity Level selects 20, 40, or 60 Districts, corresponding approximately to 10%, 20%, or 30% of current Districts; 40 Districts is the central display scenario. The worker-share capacity sensitivity selects Districts in priority order until 10%, 20%, or 30% of Weighted Working-Age Scale is reached. For rectangular comparison in Targeting Strategy Performance across Capacity Levels, A0 and A1 may be repeated at each capacity tier as fixed reference rows, but their coverage values do not change with that tier.

### Identification Assumptions

1. Nominal Survey Year and released Survey Month provide a consistent month-level exposure index across waves, although they do not identify an interview day.
2. The matched month and immediately preceding month form a comparable exposure window across waves.
3. The exploratory person-level association is interpreted only after the frozen timing, specificity, stability, and multiplicity checks; it is not treated as the causal effect of humid heat.
4. Person Weight provides a defensible basis for relative national working-age and Low Education summaries. Every district decision input first aggregates available months within District and Survey Wave and then averages observed Survey Waves equally, preventing waves with larger samples or more released survey months from mechanically dominating the district indicators.
5. Current District boundaries are acceptable operational units for an illustrative district-targeted program, although historical boundary changes and within-district heterogeneity remain unresolved.
6. The selected District count is an illustrative capacity unit rather than an observed budget or cost function. Results must be supplemented by the worker-share capacity sensitivity.
7. Higher District Low-Education Share indicates fewer average educational resources for understanding, negotiating, or adopting protection guidance; it is not the causal effect of schooling and is not a complete vulnerability index.
8. Historical humid-heat burden, working-age scale, and vulnerable-worker scale remain decision-relevant for prospective screening. No future-climate or population projection is implied.
9. Equal component weights are a transparent compromise convention rather than an objectively correct social-welfare function. Pure and emphasis weights expose the consequences of alternative priorities.
10. Data Support Grade affects confidence and sensitivity eligibility but not the substantive score, preventing sparse data from being mistaken for either low or high social priority.
11. The 26 C reference gives the continuous decision sensitivity a common substantive zero with the threshold definition. Only positive Two-Month Average Maximum Wet-Bulb Temperature excess contributes; the absolute Celsius origin never enters District Priority Score.
12. Structural robustness and wave-composition robustness answer different questions. The deterministic scenario universe varies burden definition, weights, leave-one-wave-out construction, and eligibility; the bootstrap fixes the primary threshold burden and equal-weight A5 rule and resamples only Survey Waves.

### Interpretation Boundary

The baseline model supports an exploratory district-month repeated-cross-section association, not an exact past-seven-day physiological shock or a causal heat effect. The impact-assessment layer supports comparative statements such as which targeting rule covers more observed humid-heat burden or a larger share of low-education working-age people under a stated capacity. It does not identify the realized effectiveness, avoided labor loss, health benefit, compliance response, or monetary value of the proposed program.

The national Work Participation coefficient is not converted into district-specific loss or avoided-impact estimates. With a common national coefficient, such a conversion would add no independent district ordering beyond exposure burden and would create false precision because the association is exploratory and does not survive global false-discovery control. The coefficient is retained only as evidence for the plausibility and direction of the impact pathway.

Priority maps show relative screening results under current boundaries and assumptions. They do not show district-specific causal effects, household exposure, or a mandate to exclude unselected Districts from basic heat protection. Limited Data Support Grade identifies where better local information or staged monitoring should accompany implementation.

### Relationship to Final Outputs

- The six completed figures and eleven completed tables preserve the empirical baseline, spatial pattern, education contrast, robustness checks, null findings, and multiplicity boundary.
- Occupational Humid-Heat Protection Impact-Assessment Framework makes the two-layer evidence chain and causal boundary explicit.
- Proposed Occupational Humid-Heat Protection Alternatives and Assessment Criteria freezes the action alternatives and assessment criteria before comparison.
- From District Conditions to Robust Protection Priorities and District Priority Rankings and Decision Robustness report how the component indicators produce priorities, separate structural from wave-composition stability, and show how Data Support Grade qualifies confidence.
- Coverage, Equity, and Capacity Trade-offs across Targeting Strategies and Targeting Strategy Performance across Capacity Levels compare the observable consequences of alternative allocation rules.

## 6. Main Estimation Framework

Notation is consistent across all formulas: previously defined symbols are reused, and each paragraph defines only newly appearing symbols.

### Frozen Person-Level Baseline

\[
Y_{igmy} = \beta CumH_{gmy} + \rho Rain_{gmy} + X_i'\gamma + \alpha_{gm} + \tau_y + \epsilon_{igmy}.
\]

Here, \(Y_{igmy}\) is Work Participation for person \(i\) in District \(g\), Survey Month \(m\), and Survey Year \(y\); \(CumH_{gmy}\) is Five-Day Cumulative Humid-Heat Exposure; \(Rain_{gmy}\) is Monthly Rainfall in 100 mm units; \(X_i\) contains Age, Age Squared, Female, and Rural; \(\alpha_{gm}\) is the District Calendar Month fixed effect; \(\tau_y\) is the Survey Year fixed effect; \(\epsilon_{igmy}\) is the error term; and \(\beta\), \(\rho\), and \(\gamma\) are the corresponding association coefficients. The primary sample uses ages 15-64 with positive Person Weight and complete model variables, survey-weighted estimation, and District-clustered uncertainty. Replacing \(CumH_{gmy}\) with Two-Month Average Maximum Wet-Bulb Temperature provides the frozen cross-definition replication.

The education-difference model is:

\[
Y_{igmy} = \beta CumHeat_{gmy} + \theta LowEdu_i + \delta(CumHeat_{gmy} \times LowEdu_i) + \rho Rain_{gmy} + X_i'\gamma + \alpha_{gm} + \tau_y + \epsilon_{igmy}.
\]

The newly appearing \(CumHeat_{gmy}\) is either Five-Day Cumulative Humid-Heat Exposure or Two-Month Average Maximum Wet-Bulb Temperature; \(LowEdu_i\) is Low Education; \(\theta\) is its conditional level association; and \(\delta\) is the difference between low-education and higher-education cumulative heat slopes. This model supplies vulnerability context, not a causal education mechanism. Timing, future-exposure placebo, dry-heat comparison, leave-one-wave-out, sample-restriction, clustering, and multiplicity checks remain frozen exactly as documented by the completed outputs.

### District Humid-Heat Burden Construction

For each available District-by-Survey-Wave-by-Survey-Month cell \(c\), define nonnegative continuous wet-bulb excess as:

\[
X_{gc}^{C,26} = \max\left(CumMeanWB_{gc} - 26, 0\right).
\]

The newly appearing \(X_{gc}^{C,26}\) is the positive part of Two-Month Average Maximum Wet-Bulb Temperature above 26 C. A value of zero therefore denotes no continuous excess above the prespecified reference rather than zero degrees Celsius.

Construct the primary threshold and replication continuous burdens as:

\[
B_g^T = E_g\left(\frac{1}{|W_g|}\sum_{y \in W_g}\frac{1}{|C_{gy}|}\sum_{c \in C_{gy}} CumD_{gc}^{26}\right),
\qquad
B_g^C = E_g\left(\frac{1}{|W_g|}\sum_{y \in W_g}\frac{1}{|C_{gy}|}\sum_{c \in C_{gy}} X_{gc}^{C,26}\right).
\]

The newly appearing \(W_g\) is the set of observed Survey Waves for District \(g\), and \(C_{gy}\) is the set of available Survey Month cells for District \(g\) in Survey Wave \(y\). The nested mean first averages available months within a District-wave and then gives each observed Survey Wave equal weight. \(B_g^T\) is District Threshold Humid-Heat Burden; \(B_g^C\) is the corrected District Continuous Humid-Heat Burden; \(E_g\) is Weighted Working-Age Scale; and \(CumD_{gc}^{26}\) is Two-Month Cumulative Extreme Humid-Heat Days. The threshold burden is primary; the corrected continuous burden is a replication sensitivity and must never be calculated from absolute Celsius temperature.

### District Rank Transformation and Priority Score

For any district indicator \(Q\), calculate its percentile-rank transformation as:

\[
R_g(Q) = \frac{\operatorname{rank}_g(Q)-1}{G-1}.
\]

The newly appearing \(R_g(Q)\) is the percentile rank of \(Q\) for District \(g\), \(\operatorname{rank}_g(Q)\) is its ascending average rank with ties receiving their mean rank, and \(G\) is the number of eligible Districts. Higher values always indicate higher priority on that component.

For exposure definition \(e\) and weight vector \(w\), calculate District Priority Score as:

\[
P_g(w,e) = w_B R_g(B_g^e) + w_E R_g(E_g) + w_L R_g(L_g),
\qquad w_B + w_E + w_L = 1.
\]

The newly appearing \(P_g(w,e)\) is the scenario-specific District Priority Score; \(B_g^e\) is District Threshold Humid-Heat Burden when \(e=T\) and corrected nonnegative-excess District Continuous Humid-Heat Burden when \(e=C\); \(E_g\) is Weighted Working-Age Scale; \(L_g\) is District Vulnerable-Worker Scale; and \(w_B\), \(w_E\), and \(w_L\) are their nonnegative weights. The combined rule uses equal weights as a transparent compromise. The structural sensitivity set uses burden only, worker scale only, vulnerable-worker scale only, burden emphasis, worker-scale emphasis, vulnerable-worker emphasis, and equal weights. Data Support Grade is absent from the equation.

### Selection Rules and Capacity

For targeted strategy \(s\), select:

\[
A_s(k) = \operatorname{Top}_k(Q_{gs}),
\qquad k \in \{20,40,60\}.
\]

The newly appearing \(A_s(k)\) is the set of Districts selected by Targeting Strategy \(s\) at District-count Capacity Level \(k\), and \(Q_{gs}\) is the ranking quantity for that strategy: District Threshold Humid-Heat Burden for A2, District Vulnerable-Worker Scale for A3, Weighted Working-Age Scale for A4, and the equal-weight District Priority Score for A5. Ties are resolved reproducibly by District after the substantive ranking quantity. A0 selects the empty set and A1 selects all eligible Districts.

For worker-share target \(q\), select the shortest ranked prefix that reaches the target:

\[
A_s(q) = \operatorname{Top}_{r_s(q)}(Q_{gs}),
\qquad
r_s(q) = \min\left\{r:\sum_{g \in \operatorname{Top}_r(Q_{gs})}E_g \ge q\right\},
\qquad q \in \{0.10,0.20,0.30\}.
\]

The newly appearing \(A_s(q)\) is the worker-share-capacity selected set and \(r_s(q)\) is the number of Districts required by strategy \(s\) to reach target \(q\). The same deterministic tie rule applies.

### Coverage and Distributional Consequences

For any selected set \(A\), calculate:

\[
C_H(A) = \frac{\sum_{g \in A} E_g \overline{D}_g}{\sum_g E_g \overline{D}_g},
\qquad
C_V(A) = \frac{\sum_{g \in A} E_g V_g}{\sum_g E_g V_g},
\qquad
C_E(A) = \sum_{g \in A} E_g.
\]

The newly appearing \(C_H(A)\) is Humid-Heat Coverage, \(\overline{D}_g\) is the wave-balanced District mean of Two-Month Cumulative Extreme Humid-Heat Days defined by the nested mean above, \(C_V(A)\) is Vulnerable-Worker Coverage, and \(C_E(A)\) is Working-Age Coverage. A0 has zero coverage and A1 has unit coverage by construction. Targeted strategies are compared at the same District-count capacity and again at matched worker-share targets. By construction, A2, A3, and A4 maximize \(C_H(A)\), \(C_V(A)\), and \(C_E(A)\), respectively, among fixed-count selections. The coverage-equity frontier contains scenarios for which no other scenario has at least as much Humid-Heat Coverage and Vulnerable-Worker Coverage and strictly more of at least one. This frontier is descriptive and does not assign a monetary social-welfare weight.

### Decision Robustness and Priority Inclusion Frequency

For deterministic structural scenario universe \(r=1,\ldots,R\), calculate:

\[
\pi_g^{S}(k) = \frac{1}{N_g^{S}}\sum_{r:g \in U_r} 1\{g \in A_r(k)\}.
\]

The newly appearing \(\pi_g^{S}(k)\) is structural Priority Inclusion Frequency for District \(g\) at Capacity Level \(k\); \(U_r\) is the eligible District set in structural scenario \(r\); \(A_r(k)\) is its selected set; \(N_g^{S}\) is the number of structural scenarios in which District \(g\) is eligible; and \(R=420\). The deterministic universe crosses two prespecified humid-heat-burden definitions, seven component-weight rules, the full construction plus nine leave-one-wave-out constructions, and three eligibility definitions: all Districts, exclusion of Limited Data Support Grade, and exclusion of Coastal Climate Fallback. Eligibility restrictions affect \(N_g^{S}\) rather than entering the score. The same structural audit also records worker-share selections at \(q=0.10\), \(q=0.20\), and \(q=0.30\).

Separately, for reproducibly seeded bootstrap replicate \(j=1,\ldots,J\), calculate:

\[
\pi_g^{W}(k) = \frac{1}{N_g^{W}}\sum_{j:g \in U_j} 1\{g \in A_{5j}^{T}(k)\},
\qquad J=1000.
\]

The newly appearing \(\pi_g^{W}(k)\) is wave-bootstrap inclusion frequency; \(N_g^{W}\) is the number of bootstrap replicates in which District \(g\) has sufficient inputs; and \(A_{5j}^{T}(k)\) is the A5 selected set after resampling the nine Survey Waves with replacement, rebuilding the district indicators, holding District Threshold Humid-Heat Burden fixed as the burden definition, and holding \(w_B=w_E=w_L=1/3\). No burden-definition alternation, weight cycling, leave-one-wave-out rule, or eligibility restriction is introduced inside this bootstrap. It isolates wave-composition and temporal-support sensitivity and is not a complete design-based survey variance estimator.

A stable priority core requires structural Priority Inclusion Frequency \(\pi_g^{S}(k)\) of at least 0.80 and Data Support Grade other than Limited. Frequencies from 0.20 through less than 0.80 define decision-sensitive borderline Districts; lower frequencies indicate Districts seldom selected under the admitted structural scenarios. Wave-bootstrap inclusion frequency \(\pi_g^{W}(k)\) is reported alongside, but it does not get averaged with or substituted for \(\pi_g^{S}(k)\). These labels describe robustness, not need in an absolute ethical sense.

### Robustness and Sensitivity Set

- Preserve every completed person-level timing, placebo, dry-heat, common-sample, leave-one-wave-out, geographic, clustering, education-interaction, and multiplicity result without reselecting the baseline story.
- Recompute district indicators after excluding each Survey Wave, and audit whether changing historical coverage materially alters District Priority Score or Priority Inclusion Frequency.
- Repeat every targeted allocation with District Threshold Humid-Heat Burden and the corrected nonnegative 26 C wet-bulb-excess District Continuous Humid-Heat Burden.
- Compare equal weights with the six prespecified pure or emphasis weight rules; do not optimize weights against a preferred observed outcome.
- Repeat allocations after excluding Limited Data Support Grade and Coastal Climate Fallback, while keeping Data Support Grade outside the score.
- Compare District-count capacity with worker-share capacity to expose the consequences of treating administrative units as equally costly.
- Use the Survey Wave block bootstrap as a separate sensitivity to historical-wave composition with threshold burden and equal A5 weights fixed; do not mix it with the structural scenario frequency or label it a full survey-design confidence interval.
- Report coverage consequences for every admitted scenario, including unfavorable or dominated combined-strategy results.

### Reporting and Evidence Rules

- Retain estimates, 95% confidence intervals, exact values, sample sizes, District clusters, fixed effects, controls, weights, and multiplicity results for the frozen person-level baseline.
- Label the cumulative association and education difference exploratory and report that no eligible test survives global 10% false-discovery control.
- For every structural allocation scenario, report Targeting Strategy, Capacity Level, eligible and selected District counts, Humid-Heat Coverage, Vulnerable-Worker Coverage, Working-Age Coverage, burden definition, weight rule, construction rule, and Data Support Grade composition.
- Present District Threshold Humid-Heat Burden as primary and District Continuous Humid-Heat Burden as replication; do not select whichever yields the more attractive ranking.
- Report structural and wave-bootstrap inclusion frequencies in separately labelled fields or workbook sheets and state exactly which uncertainty source each represents.
- Report A0 and A1 as lower and upper reference points, not as equally feasible alternatives under limited capacity.
- Report stable, borderline, and evidence-limited Districts together. Do not suppress a high-priority District because its Data Support Grade is Limited; flag it for validation and monitoring.
- Do not report avoided jobs, causal program benefits, cost-effectiveness, or future impacts from the observational coefficient.
